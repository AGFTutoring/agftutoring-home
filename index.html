About Alastair Fisher

The educator behind
AGF Tutoring.

My work over the past thirty years has centred on a single question: how students come to understand difficult ideas clearly, and how that clarity translates into performance under examination conditions.

My academic background combines the sciences with a longstanding interest in language, cognition and the way people learn. That combination has shaped a method that is both analytical and diagnostic: I am interested not merely in what a student gets wrong, but in where understanding first begins to slip.

Most academic difficulty does not arise from lack of ability. It arises from confusion left unresolved — often small at first, but increasingly costly under pressure. My work is to identify those points of divergence, rebuild understanding from first principles, and translate that understanding into precise, confident performance.

Beyond the lesson

The aim is not simply higher grades, though grades matter. It is the development of a more stable and exact way of thinking. That is why my work frequently extends beyond a single subject into academic mentoring, university choices, applications and longer-term educational strategy.

  • More than 30 years of private tutoring and academic mentoring
  • Extensive experience with UK, US and European university pathways
  • Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry across major international qualifications
  • Admissions tests, applications, interviews and scholarship strategy

The AGF Method

Teaching as a
diagnostic process.

The method is simple in outline but exacting in practice. The visible mistake is often only the last link in a much longer chain.

01

Diagnose

Find where understanding first breaks down, not simply where the final answer goes wrong.

02

Rebuild

Restore the concept from first principles, using intuition before procedure.

03

Clarify

Introduce exact language, formal structure and disciplined written expression.

04

Test

Use carefully chosen questions to establish whether the idea is genuinely secure.

05

Extend

Move from competence to mastery, particularly with unfamiliar questions and pressure.

Example · Chemistry
Why is the bond angle in H₂O smaller than in NH₃?
Before applying VSEPR mechanically, compare the number of lone pairs. The key missing idea is that lone pair–lone pair repulsion is stronger than lone pair–bond pair repulsion. That additional repulsion compresses the H–O–H bond angle more strongly.
Next question: can you now predict the relative bond angle in CH₄ and explain why?
